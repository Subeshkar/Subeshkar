Privacy policy

Last updated 15 November 2026

Dioma is a personal assistant. This page explains what we collect, why, and the control you have. Short version: your data is used only to run Dioma for you. We do not sell it, we do not use it for advertising, and you can delete everything at any time.

What we store

Your email address and the name and description you give us; your to-dos; your conversations with Dioma; files you upload; and, if you connect Google, an encrypted access token for your account.

Google data

If you connect Google, Dioma asks for read-only access to Gmail and Google Calendar. Dioma reads the sender, subject and a short preview of recent unread email, and today's calendar events, to write your daily brief and answer your questions. Dioma cannot send, change or delete your email or events. We do not store the contents of your email; we fetch what is needed when you open Dioma and keep it in memory for a few minutes.

Dioma's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We do not use Google data for advertising, do not sell it, do not let people read it unless you ask us to for support or the law requires it, and do not use it to train general AI models.

AI processing

Dioma's answers are generated by Claude, an AI model from Anthropic. When you ask Dioma something or open your brief, the relevant context (your question, to-dos, and the mail and calendar details above) is sent to Anthropic's API to produce the answer. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, this data is not used to train their models.

Other services we use

Railway (hosting and storage) and Resend (sending your sign-in codes). Each only processes data as needed to provide that service.

Your control

Disconnect Google at any time in Me, which also revokes Dioma's access with Google. Delete account in Me removes your account, to-dos, chats and files immediately. You can also manage access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Security

Connections are encrypted (HTTPS). Google tokens are stored encrypted. Sign-in uses one-time codes; we never store passwords.
